## What is the ICC?

The **International Cybersecurity Challenge (ICC)** is a global youth cybersecurity competition created by a consortium including ENISA (EU), Code Blue (Japan), Katzcy (USA), and NUST (Africa). One team per continent. The top young players from each region, selected through a rigorous national and regional process based on performance records, CTF specialties, and competitive track record throughout the year.

You don't sign up. You get picked.

### PCAP Analyzer for Team AFRICC

I developed [PCAP Analyzer](https://github.com/en2eav0r/PCAP-Analyzer) for Team AFRICC to use during ICC 2025 in Japan. The Python tool rebuilds TCP streams and extracts useful evidence from packet captures, including HTTP requests, database queries, credentials and suspicious payloads. The goal was to reduce the time spent searching through large captures by hand during network forensics challenges.

### Day 3: Attack & Defense CTF

Teams defend their own services while attacking opponents' infrastructure at the same time. No rotation, no breaks. The most demanding format in competitive cybersecurity.

## What this meant

Getting selected for the ICC put me among the top young cybersecurity players on the African continent, under 26.

> *"If you want to be the best, you need to face the best, learn from them, push yourself to their level, and eventually surpass them."*

When I came back, I redesigned the IPNET CYBER CLUB's methodology around that principle.

## Bringing the experience home

ICC put me in a team of 17 players from more than 10 countries. We had to work across specialties and national backgrounds, then switch from Jeopardy to Attack & Defense. In the second format, an exploit is only half the work: your own services must stay online while the other teams attack them.

Back at IPNET, I changed parts of the [IPNET CYBER CLUB](/posts/ipnet-cybersec-club/) training around what I had seen in Tokyo. Members needed practice outside their preferred category. They also had to explain solutions to the group and learn to coordinate under time pressure. I did not want the trip to end as one more line on my résumé.

The result belongs to Team Africa. This page records my selection and participation without attributing the team's collective work to one player.
